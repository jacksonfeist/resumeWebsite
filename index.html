01

Start with a pilot

Almost every job with me starts the same way. You pick one task that eats the most time, and I build the piece of software that does it. It is small enough to finish quickly and small enough for you to judge the result yourself instead of taking my word for it.

  1. 01

    Fixed scope, fixed price, one to two weeks

    Before I start, I write down exactly what the software will do and quote one price for it. The price does not move after that, and the work is finished inside one to two weeks.

  2. 02

    It runs on a copy of your data

    I work from a copy of your records, never the originals. Nothing touches the system you use every day until you have watched the new thing do the job correctly and told me you are satisfied with it.

  3. 03

    If it does not save the hours I expect, you do not pay for phase two

    I tell you up front how much time a week I expect this to give back. If the pilot does not deliver that, there is no second phase to pay for. You keep what was built, and that is where it ends.

  4. 04

    The last 10 to 15 percent still needs a person

    In this kind of work there are always cases the software should not decide on its own. Usually 10 to 15 percent of them. Those get set aside on a short list for a person to handle, rather than guessed at.

Tell me the one task you would hand off first. If a pilot is the wrong fit for it, I will say so and tell you what I would do instead.

Boon.art

Artists lose hours every week hunting for grants, residencies, and open calls one organization at a time. Boon gathers all of it into one place and keeps the list up to date on its own, so nobody has to go collect it by hand and deadlines stop slipping by unnoticed.

I designed and built it for a nonprofit client while I was a product team lead at Oregon Software Consulting. The site is public and running now. This is the closest thing on the page to the work I get asked for most: a job that used to be done by hand, now done by software.

In Marketplace review

Jira app / Atlassian Forge

Secure Fields for Jira

Teams in healthcare, legal, and finance often have to track work in the same place as sensitive information that most of their coworkers are not allowed to see. Secure Fields keeps those values visible only to the people and roles you name, and it checks every single request against that list instead of trusting the screen it came from.

I built it on my own: the app itself, the security testing, and the store listing. It is finished and currently in review at the Atlassian Marketplace, so it is not available to install yet.
